## How phishing works
Phishing messages imitate banks, delivery companies, streaming services or government portals. They create a problem — a blocked account, failed delivery or pending KYC — and offer a link that fixes it. The link leads to a copy of a real login page that sends everything you type to the criminal.

## Why urgency is the main tactic
Deadlines like "within 24 hours" or "account blocked tonight" are designed to stop you from pausing to check. Real organisations give notice through their official app and do not threaten immediate closure over SMS.

## Warning signs
- Generic greetings such as "Dear customer"
- Threats to block, suspend or disconnect your account
- Links that are shortened or don't match the company's real domain
- Requests to "update", "verify" or "confirm" details through a link

## Checking a link safely
Look at the part just before the first single slash: that is the real website. "sbi.co.in.verify-kyc.xyz" belongs to verify-kyc.xyz, not SBI. When in doubt, open the organisation's official app yourself instead of the link.

## What to do
Don't click. If you already entered a password, change it immediately from the official app and enable two-step verification. Report the message through the Sanchar Saathi "Chakshu" facility or at cybercrime.gov.in.
