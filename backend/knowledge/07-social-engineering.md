## Impersonating people you trust
"Hi Mom, I lost my phone, this is my new number" and "This is your CEO, buy gift cards for a client" are common scripts. They rely on you wanting to help quickly. The fix is simple: call the person on the number you already have before doing anything.

## Fear, greed and secrecy
Social engineering works by triggering strong emotions — fear of arrest, excitement about a prize, sympathy for an emergency — and then isolating you ("don't tell anyone", "stay on the call"). Any request to keep something secret from your family is a red flag.

## Remote-access apps
Fake tech-support and bank agents ask you to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer or a "support" app. These apps show your screen to the caller and can reveal OTPs and banking apps. No bank needs remote access to your phone.

## Prizes you never entered
You can't win a lottery, lucky draw or KBC prize you didn't enter. These messages always lead to a "processing fee", "GST" or a request for bank details.

## Extortion threats
If someone threatens to share private photos or videos unless you pay, don't pay: payment leads to more demands. Keep the evidence, block the account and report at cybercrime.gov.in.
