## Look-alike domains
Scam sites register names that look like real ones: "paypa1.com" with the number one, "amaz0n-refund.com", or "hdfc-netbanking-update.xyz". A brand name appearing somewhere in a link does not make it official — only the registered domain matters.

## The padlock myth
HTTPS and the padlock icon mean the connection is encrypted, not that the site is honest. Most phishing sites today use HTTPS. A missing padlock is a warning sign, but its presence proves nothing.

## Free hosting and cheap domain endings
Website builders and free hosting platforms are legitimate, but criminals use them to create throwaway pages in minutes. Domain endings like .xyz, .top or .online are cheap and heavily abused. Neither proves a site is fake, but combined with a brand name or login form they are a strong warning sign.

## Links that download apps
A link ending in .apk installs an Android app from outside the Play Store. Scam apps can read your SMS, capture OTPs and let criminals control your phone. Install apps only from official app stores.

## What to do
Type the official address yourself or use the official app. If you entered card details on a suspicious site, block the card through your bank immediately.
