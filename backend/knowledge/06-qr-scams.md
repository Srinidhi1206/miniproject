## "Scan to receive" is always a lie
A UPI QR code contains the details of the person who will receive money. When you scan it and enter your PIN, you pay them. Buyers on marketplaces who say "scan this so I can pay you" are stealing from you.

## QR codes that hide links
QR codes can contain any web address. Fake parking-fine stickers, restaurant menus or "claim your reward" posters can open phishing pages. Because you can't read a QR code with your eyes, always check the address your phone shows before opening it.

## Warning signs
- A stranger sends you a QR code over chat to "receive" a payment
- The amount is pre-filled but you are supposed to be the one receiving
- A sticker pasted over an original QR code in a shop or parking lot
- The payee name on your UPI app doesn't match the business

## What to do
Only scan to pay someone you chose to pay, and check the payee name and amount on the confirmation screen before entering your PIN. Ask buyers to pay you directly to your UPI ID instead.
