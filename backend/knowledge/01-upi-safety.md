## The one rule that stops most UPI fraud
Your UPI PIN is only used to send money out of your account. You never need to enter a PIN, scan a QR code or approve a request to receive money, a refund or cashback. Any message that says otherwise is a scam, because the action it asks for moves money from you to the scammer.

## How collect-request scams work
Scammers send a UPI collect request labelled "refund", "cashback" or "prize". The UPI app shows a Pay button, and once you enter your PIN the money leaves your account. The label on a request is typed by the sender and proves nothing.

## The "sent by mistake" trick
A stranger claims they transferred money to you by mistake and asks you to send it back, sometimes with a fake SMS that looks like a credit alert. Check your actual balance in your bank app. If money really arrived, your bank can reverse it through official channels — never send it back to a number the stranger gives you.

## Warning signs
- Someone asks for your UPI PIN, OTP or to "approve" a request to receive money
- A buyer on OLX or Facebook Marketplace insists on paying by QR code
- A payment note says refund, prize, cashback or KYC
- Pressure to act within minutes

## What to do
Decline unknown collect requests. Only enter your PIN when you intend to pay a person or shop you chose. If money was lost, call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in within the first hours, and inform your bank to raise a dispute.
