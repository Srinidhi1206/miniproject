## Why scammers want your OTP
A one-time password is the last step that confirms a login, payment or SIM change. A scammer who already has your card or account number only needs the OTP to complete the transaction. That is why so many scams end with "just tell me the code you received".

## Who can legitimately ask for it
Nobody. The RBI and all banks state that staff will never ask for your OTP, PIN, CVV or password — not on a call, SMS, WhatsApp or email. Delivery agents may ask for a delivery code printed on your order, but never for a banking OTP.

## Read the OTP message itself
Genuine OTP messages say what the code is for, for example "OTP for payment of Rs 49,999 to XYZ". If the purpose doesn't match what the caller told you, the caller is lying.

## Warning signs
- "I sent a code to your phone by mistake, please send it back"
- "Read out the OTP so we can cancel the fraudulent transaction"
- Requests for card number, expiry date and CVV together

## What to do
Never read out or forward an OTP. If you already shared one, call your bank immediately to block the account and cards, then report at 1930.
